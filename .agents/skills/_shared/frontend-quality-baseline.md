# Frontend Quality Baseline

Apply this baseline whenever any repository frontend skill is active. Preserve the user's requested direction and the portfolio's own identity; named products are quality references, never templates to imitate.

## Product standard

- Aim for the execution discipline associated with Linear, Stripe, Vercel, Apple, Notion, Framer, Raycast, and high-end independent portfolios without copying their visual language.
- Make content, audience, and task hierarchy drive the interface. Decoration must clarify brand, structure, state, or interaction.
- Prefer a small coherent system of intentional choices over a large catalog of effects or components.
- Treat desktop, mobile, accessibility, loading behavior, and reduced motion as parts of the design, not cleanup work.
- Preserve the existing stack unless the task proves a new dependency is necessary: Next.js, React, TypeScript, Tailwind CSS, and Motion.

## Anti-vibecode constraints

Do not default to:

- gradients, glassmorphism, glow, blurred color blobs, or deep shadows as substitutes for composition;
- wrapping every section in a rounded card or using pills for ordinary labels and links;
- oversized generic hero copy, centered marketing-page sameness, or default three-column SaaS grids;
- meaningless statistics, badges, testimonials, logo walls, dashboards, or decorative controls;
- emojis as interface icons, arbitrary icon mixes, or decoration with no information value;
- repeated card patterns, excessive rounding, unexplained visual noise, or random animation;
- invented content, claims, metrics, clients, testimonials, project outcomes, or capabilities.

Prefer:

- strong type, deliberate whitespace, clear alignment, and content-led composition;
- restrained color and motion, purposeful asymmetry, and high-quality project imagery;
- semantic markup, visible states, honest content, and reusable design primitives;
- distinctive decisions that can be explained in terms of the visitor, content, and portfolio story.

## Evidence before invention

Before changing UI, inspect the relevant route, components, styles, content, assets, existing primitives, and responsive behavior. Reuse sound local patterns. Do not erase an intentional design direction merely because another direction is fashionable.

When a request leaves a consequential brand, content, or interaction choice unresolved, make the smallest reversible assumption and state it. Never fabricate proof or business facts to complete a layout.

## Completion gate

A frontend task is not complete until the implementation has been checked at representative narrow, medium, and wide viewports; keyboard and focus behavior; reduced motion where animation exists; content wrapping and overflow; and every state introduced by the change. Use browser verification when available and proportionate to the task.
