# UX User Flow and Experience Architecture

Read [the shared frontend quality baseline](../_shared/frontend-quality-baseline.md) before making decisions.

## Purpose

Shape the portfolio so a visitor can quickly understand who the owner is, what they do, why their work is credible, and how to take the next step.

## Principles

- Information architecture should answer visitor questions in a persuasive, evidence-based sequence.
- Strong project proof is more valuable than repeated claims, decorative metrics, or exhaustive technology lists.
- Navigation should reduce uncertainty and preserve orientation without competing with the work.
- Every CTA needs a plausible visitor intent and a destination that fulfills its promise.
- Remove repetition and dead ends before adding interactions to compensate for them.

## Implementation rules

1. Identify target visitors, their likely evaluation questions, the primary portfolio goal, and the available truthful proof.
2. Evaluate the core journey: arrival → identity → capability → experience proof → strong projects → technical credibility → professional trust → contact.
3. Treat that sequence as a diagnostic model, not a mandatory section template. Reorder or combine beats when the actual content tells a clearer story.
4. Give each page or section one primary job. Place context before proof that needs it and place CTAs near the confidence-building content that supports them.
5. Use project narratives to show problem, role, constraints, decisions, execution, and verified outcome when those facts exist.
6. Keep global navigation concise, labels unambiguous, anchors accurate, and contact/resume destinations complete. Provide a next step at the end of deep content.
7. Test the flow as a first-time recruiter or collaborator, on a quick scan and a deeper read, then remove repeated claims and unexplained detours.

## Anti-patterns

- A visually impressive opening that does not explain identity or professional value.
- Generic section sequences copied from SaaS landing pages or portfolio templates.
- Invented metrics, testimonials, clients, outcomes, or social proof.
- Repeating the same biography, skills, or CTA in multiple sections without advancing the story.
- Technology logo walls without context about how the tools were used.
- Project cards that show only screenshots and tags, with no role, challenge, or decision evidence.
- Navigation items that lead to sparse sections, broken anchors, or dead ends.

## Review checklist

- [ ] A first-time visitor can state who this person is and what they do after the opening.
- [ ] The sequence moves from orientation to credible proof and a clear next action.
- [ ] Projects communicate role, decisions, execution, and truthful outcomes where available.
- [ ] Navigation and CTAs have clear labels, useful destinations, and appropriate placement.
- [ ] Repetition, cognitive load, unclear copy, unnecessary interactions, and dead ends are removed.
- [ ] Both quick scanning and deeper evaluation paths work on desktop and mobile.
